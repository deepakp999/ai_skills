# Marketing Management

A structured way to create, communicate and deliver customer value profitably, adapted from *Marketing Management* (Kotler, Keller; later editions with Chernev). Works for consumer, B2B, service, digital/AI and internal products. Pairs with the `product-design-development` suite: that suite builds the right product; this skill takes it to market.

**Core logic:** marketing is a value-delivery process. **Choose the value** (research, segment, target, position) → **provide the value** (product, service, price, channels) → **communicate the value** (brand, communications, digital, sales) → **grow and sustain the value** (growth strategy, loyalty, metrics, responsibility). Strategy (who and why) comes before tactics (the 4Ps).

## How to run this skill
1. **Locate the user**: new product launch, repositioning, growth stall, pricing question, channel conflict, campaign, annual marketing plan?
2. **Check the strategic foundation first**: do they know their target segment, the need, the competitive frame and the value proposition? If not, start at Part B before tactics. Tactics without positioning is the most common failure.
3. **Use the tool for the current question**, fill templates with real data; mark guesses `[assumption — validate]`.
4. **Close with metrics**: how will we know it worked (Part F)?
5. Offer to assemble everything into a **marketing plan** (template at the end).
Right-size: a startup needs one-page versions; a large launch needs the full plan.

## Part A — Understand the market and the customer

**Marketing concepts**: needs (basic requirements) → wants (needs shaped by context) → demand (wants backed by ability to pay). Value = benefits ÷ costs as the customer perceives them. Satisfaction = performance vs expectations. Exchange, relationships, networks; markets (consumer, business, global, non-profit/government).
**Marketing orientations**: production → product → selling → marketing (find the right products for customers) → **holistic marketing** = relationship marketing + integrated marketing + internal marketing (everyone in the firm) + performance marketing (financial and social results).

**Tool A1 — Marketing environment scan**
- **Macro (PESTEL)**: political-legal, economic, social-cultural/demographic, technological, environmental, legal. For each: trend → opportunity/threat → implication.
- **Micro**: company, suppliers, intermediaries, customers, competitors, publics.
- **SWOT** → strategic options (use strengths on opportunities; fix weaknesses blocking opportunities; defend against threats).

**Tool A2 — Marketing research process**: define problem & objectives → develop plan (secondary data, observation, ethnography, focus groups, surveys, behavioural/digital data, experiments) → collect → analyze → present findings → decide. Build a marketing information system (internal records, intelligence, research, analytics) and **demand forecasts** (market potential, market share, buying-intention surveys, sales-force composite, expert opinion, time-series, test markets).

**Tool A3 — Consumer buying behaviour**
- Influences: cultural (culture, subculture, class), social (reference groups, family, roles/status), personal (age, life stage, occupation, income, lifestyle, personality, values), psychological (motivation — Maslow/Herzberg, perception, learning, memory, emotions).
- **Five-stage decision process**: problem recognition → information search → evaluation of alternatives (attributes, beliefs, expectancy-value) → purchase decision (others' attitudes, unanticipated factors, perceived risk) → post-purchase behaviour (satisfaction, use, disposal). Add **customer journey mapping** with touchpoints.
- Behavioural decision biases: heuristics (availability, representativeness, anchoring), framing, mental accounting.

**Tool A4 — Business (B2B) buying**
- Buying situations: straight rebuy, modified rebuy, new task.
- **Buying centre** roles: initiator, user, influencer, decider, approver, buyer, gatekeeper — map them for each account.
- Stages: problem recognition → general need → product specification → supplier search → proposal solicitation → supplier selection → order routine → performance review.
- Build trust and relationships; manage risk and value-in-use; solution selling; institutional and government buyers.

**Tool A5 — Competitive analysis**
- Identify competitors by **industry** and by **market (customer need)** — include substitutes.
- Porter's five forces (rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, buyer power, supplier power).
- Analyse each competitor's strategy, objectives, strengths/weaknesses (share of market, share of mind, share of heart).
- **Competitive positions & strategies**: leader (expand total market, defend share — position, flank, pre-emptive, counteroffensive, mobile, contraction defence; expand share), challenger (frontal, flank, encirclement, bypass, guerrilla attacks), follower (counterfeiter, cloner, imitator, adapter), nicher (specialize by end user, customer size, geography, product, service, channel).
- Balance customer orientation and competitor orientation.

## Part B — Strategy: choose the value (STP)

**Tool B1 — Segmentation**
| Base | Variables |
|---|---|
| Geographic | Region, city size, density, climate |
| Demographic | Age, life stage, gender, income, generation, social class, ethnicity |
| Psychographic | Lifestyle, values, personality |
| Behavioural | Occasions, benefits sought, user status, usage rate, loyalty, readiness stage, attitude, decision roles |
| B2B | Industry, company size, location, operating variables (technology, user status), purchasing approach, situational factors (urgency, order size), personal characteristics |
Effective segments are **measurable, substantial, accessible, differentiable, actionable**.

**Tool B2 — Targeting**: evaluate segments on size and growth, structural attractiveness (five forces), fit with objectives and resources. Patterns: single-segment concentration, selective specialization, product specialization, market specialization, full coverage (undifferentiated or differentiated); micromarketing (local, individual/customized). Consider ethics of targeting vulnerable groups.

**Tool B3 — Positioning**
- Define the **competitive frame of reference** (category membership, which competitors).
- **Points of parity (POPs)**: category POPs, correlational POPs, competitive POPs — "good enough" on what customers expect.
- **Points of difference (PODs)**: attributes/benefits customers strongly associate with the brand, evaluate positively, and believe can't be matched equally — must be desirable, deliverable, differentiating.
- Resolve trade-offs (e.g., low price + high quality) by separating attributes, leveraging another entity, or redefining the relationship.
- **Positioning statement**: *For [target] who [need], [brand] is the [frame of reference] that [point of difference] because [reason to believe].*
- Also: **brand mantra** (3–5 words capturing the essence), category membership communication, perceptual maps.
- Differentiation sources: product, services, personnel, channel, image; employee/channel/brand-based. Consider **Blue Ocean** moves (eliminate–reduce–raise–create) to make competition irrelevant.

**Tool B4 — Value proposition & customer value**
- Customer perceived value = total benefits (product, service, personnel, image) − total costs (monetary, time, energy, psychological).
- Value proposition = the full set of benefits promised; **value delivery system** = all experiences on the way.
- Choose a value discipline (operational excellence, customer intimacy, product leadership).

**Tool B5 — Corporate & business strategy**
- Mission, vision, values; strategic business units.
- **Portfolio**: BCG growth-share (stars, cash cows, question marks, dogs), GE/McKinsey attractiveness-strength.
- **Growth**: Ansoff (market penetration, market development, product development, diversification) — plus integrative growth (backward, forward, horizontal) and diversification (concentric, horizontal, conglomerate).
- Porter generic strategies: overall cost leadership, differentiation, focus.
- Strategic alliances; business-unit planning: mission → SWOT → goals (SMART) → strategy → programs → implementation → feedback & control.

## Part C — Brand: build and manage the value

**Tool C1 — Brand equity (customer-based)**: the differential effect brand knowledge has on response to marketing. Built through brand awareness (recall, recognition) and brand image (strong, favourable, unique associations).
**Brand resonance pyramid** (build bottom-up):
1. **Salience** — identity: who are you?
2. **Performance & imagery** — meaning: what are you? (functional / symbolic)
3. **Judgments & feelings** — response: what do I think and feel about you?
4. **Resonance** — relationship: how much connection (loyalty, attachment, community, engagement)?
**Brand elements** (name, logo, symbol, character, slogans, jingles, packaging, URL) chosen to be memorable, meaningful, likable, transferable, adaptable, protectable. Leverage secondary associations (company, country, channels, co-brands, endorsers, events, causes).
**Measuring equity**: brand audits (inventory + exploratory), tracking studies, brand value chain (marketing program → customer mindset → market performance → shareholder value).
**Managing brands**: brand architecture (branded house vs house of brands vs sub-brands), brand extensions (fit, parent strength, risks of dilution), reinforcement and revitalization over time, global brand consistency vs adaptation.

## Part D — Provide the value (offering, price, channels)

**Tool D1 — Product strategy**
- **Five product levels**: core benefit → basic product → expected product → augmented product → potential product.
- Classifications: durability/tangibility; consumer goods (convenience, shopping, specialty, unsought); industrial goods (materials/parts, capital items, supplies/services).
- Differentiation: form, features, customization, performance quality, conformance, durability, reliability, repairability, style, design.
- **Product line & mix**: width, length, depth, consistency; line stretching (up, down, both), line filling, modernizing, featuring, pruning; co-branding; packaging, labelling, warranties, guarantees.
- **New product development**: idea generation → screening → concept development & testing → marketing strategy → business analysis → product development → market testing → commercialization. Diffusion of innovations (innovators, early adopters, early majority, late majority, laggards; relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, divisibility, communicability).
- **Product life cycle** strategies: introduction (build awareness, trial), growth (expand distribution, features), maturity (modify market, product, mix; defend), decline (harvest, divest, niche).

**Tool D2 — Services**: characteristics — intangibility, inseparability, variability, perishability. Manage the **service quality gaps** (between customer expectation and management perception; perception and quality specs; specs and delivery; delivery and external communications; perceived vs expected). **SERVQUAL** dimensions: reliability, responsiveness, assurance, empathy, tangibles. Extended mix adds **people, process, physical evidence**. Self-service technology, customer empowerment, service recovery, managing supply and demand.

**Tool D3 — Pricing**
Six steps: 1 select pricing objective (survival, max current profit, max market share, market skimming, product-quality leadership) → 2 determine demand (price sensitivity, elasticity, reference prices, price-quality inferences, price endings) → 3 estimate costs (fixed, variable, experience curve, target costing) → 4 analyze competitors' costs, prices, offers → 5 select method → 6 select final price (psychological pricing, gain-and-risk sharing, company policy, impact on other parties).
Methods: markup, target-return, **perceived-value**, **value pricing** (EDLP vs high-low), going-rate, auction, dynamic/algorithmic.
Adapting price: geographic, discounts & allowances (cash, quantity, functional, seasonal), promotional pricing, differentiated pricing (segment, product-form, image, channel, location, time), freemium/subscription/bundling. Price changes: when to cut or raise, responding to competitors' price moves (maintain, raise perceived value, reduce price, raise price + quality, launch fighter brand). Watch price wars and legal limits (discrimination, predatory pricing, price-fixing).

**Tool D4 — Channels & value networks**
- Channel functions: information, promotion, negotiation, ordering, financing, risk taking, physical possession, payment, title.
- Channel levels (zero = direct, one, two, three); **multichannel / omnichannel** integration.
- Design: analyze customer service output needs (lot size, waiting time, spatial convenience, assortment, service backup) → set objectives → identify alternatives (types of intermediaries, intensive/selective/exclusive distribution, terms and responsibilities) → evaluate (economic, control, adaptive).
- Management: select, train, motivate (power: coercive, reward, legitimate, expert, referent), evaluate members; manage **channel conflict** (vertical, horizontal, multichannel) and cooperation; vertical marketing systems (corporate, administered, contractual — franchises), horizontal marketing systems.
- **Retailing, wholesaling, logistics**: store and non-store formats, e-commerce, private labels; logistics objectives (order processing, warehousing, inventory, transport) and supply-chain trade-offs.

## Part E — Communicate the value

**Tool E1 — Integrated marketing communications (IMC)**
Steps: identify target audience → determine objectives (category need, brand awareness, brand attitude, purchase intention) → design communications (message strategy, creative strategy — informational vs transformational appeals, message source credibility) → select channels (personal and non-personal) → establish total budget (affordable, percentage-of-sales, competitive parity, **objective-and-task**) → decide mix → measure results → manage integration.
**Response hierarchy models**: AIDA (attention, interest, desire, action), hierarchy-of-effects (awareness, knowledge, liking, preference, conviction, purchase), innovation-adoption.
**Communications mix**: advertising, sales promotion, events & experiences, public relations & publicity, online & social media, mobile, direct & database marketing, personal selling, word of mouth.
**Mix choice factors**: type of product market (B2C vs B2B), buyer-readiness stage, product life-cycle stage, push vs pull strategy.

**Tool E2 — Mass communications**
- **Advertising — 5 Ms**: mission (inform, persuade, remind, reinforce), money (budget factors: PLC stage, share, competition, frequency, substitutability), message (generation, evaluation, execution, social responsibility), media (reach, frequency, impact; media types, timing, allocation), measurement (communication-effect and sales-effect research).
- **Sales promotion**: consumer (samples, coupons, cash refunds, price packs, premiums, loyalty programs, contests), trade, business/sales-force tools; set objectives, select tools, develop/pretest/implement, evaluate.
- **Events & experiences**: choose events that fit target and brand; measure.
- **Public relations**: publications, events, news, community involvement, identity media, lobbying, social responsibility.

**Tool E3 — Digital communications**: owned / paid / earned media; website & content marketing, search (SEO, SEM), display, email, social media (brand presence, influencers, communities), mobile & location, online reviews, **word of mouth** (buzz, viral). Use the customer journey to map content to stages; measure engagement, conversion and attribution.

**Tool E4 — Personal communications & sales force**
- Direct marketing and CRM databases (RFM: recency, frequency, monetary value).
- **Sales force design**: objectives and strategy (selling, prospecting, targeting, communicating, servicing, information gathering, allocating), structure (territorial, product, market, complex), size (workload approach), compensation (fixed, variable, expenses, benefits).
- **Managing the sales force**: recruiting, training, supervising, motivating, evaluating.
- **Personal selling steps**: prospecting & qualifying → preapproach → presentation & demonstration (needs-satisfaction / solution selling) → overcoming objections → closing → follow-up & maintenance. Key-account management and relationship marketing for B2B.

## Part F — Grow, sustain and control the value

**Tool F1 — Customer relationships & loyalty**
- Satisfaction measurement (surveys, NPS, complaints, mystery shoppers, lost-customer analysis); total quality management.
- **Customer lifetime value (CLV)** = present value of future profits from a customer; customer profitability analysis; customer equity (value equity, brand equity, relationship equity).
- CRM: personalization, customer empowerment, reviews/recommendations; retention, frequency and club programs; win-back; reduce defection (define and measure retention, distinguish causes, estimate lost profit, cost to reduce churn, listen).
- Attract → retain → grow: acquisition funnel, cross-selling, up-selling.

**Tool F2 — Growth strategies**: Ansoff matrix (Part B5); new market entry, innovation, global expansion (decide whether to go abroad, which markets, mode of entry — indirect/direct export, licensing, joint venture, direct investment; standardize vs adapt product, communications, price, distribution; country-of-origin effects).

**Tool F3 — Socially responsible marketing**: internal marketing (organize around customers, cross-functional), ethics, corporate social responsibility, cause marketing, social marketing, sustainability, privacy and data responsibility.

**Tool F4 — Marketing metrics, implementation & control**
| Control type | Responsibility | Purpose | Tools |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual-plan control | Top & middle management | Are planned results achieved? | Sales analysis, market share analysis, sales-to-expense ratios, financial analysis, market-based scorecards |
| Profitability control | Marketing controller | Where are we making/losing money? | Profitability by product, territory, customer, segment, channel, order size |
| Efficiency control | Line & staff, controller | Spend efficiency and impact | Efficiency of sales force, advertising, promotion, distribution |
| Strategic control | Top management, auditor | Pursuing best opportunities? | Marketing effectiveness review, **marketing audit** (comprehensive, systematic, independent, periodic), marketing excellence review |
Key metrics: awareness, consideration, preference, trial/conversion, share of market/wallet/voice, customer acquisition cost (CAC), CLV, retention/churn, NPS, marketing ROI, mix-modelling and attribution, dashboards.

## Marketing plan template
```
1. Executive summary & table of contents
2. Situation analysis: market (size, growth, trends), customers & buying process, competitors, PESTEL, SWOT
3. Marketing strategy: mission & objectives (SMART), target segments, positioning statement & POPs/PODs, brand mantra, value proposition
4. Marketing mix
   - Product/offering (levels, line, roadmap)
   - Services (quality dimensions, process)
   - Price (objective, method, structure, discounts)
   - Channels (structure, partners, coverage, logistics)
   - Communications (IMC objectives, message, channels, budget, calendar; digital plan; sales force plan)
5. Financial projections: sales forecast, budget, break-even, CLV/CAC, ROI
6. Implementation: actions, owners, timeline
7. Controls: metrics dashboard, review cadence, contingency plans
```

## Working norms
- Strategy before tactics: STP and positioning before the 4Ps.
- Every tactic should trace to a segment, a need, and the positioning.
- Separate customer evidence from assumption; test with research or experiments.
- Think customer value and CLV, not just transactions.
- Consider competitor response for every major move (especially pricing).
- Integrate: all touchpoints say the same thing.

## Digital, B2B and internal-product adaptation
- **SaaS/AI products**: freemium/usage/tiered pricing; product-led growth (self-serve trials, in-product onboarding, viral loops) alongside sales-led motions; content and community marketing; CAC payback and net revenue retention as core metrics.
- **B2B/industrial (e.g., commercial vehicles)**: buying centre mapping, total cost of ownership and uptime as value proposition, dealer and service network as channel and differentiator, key-account management, fleet segments by application and duty cycle.
- **Internal tools/platforms**: "customers" are employees; segment by role and workflow; position against the current manual process; channels = rollout, champions, training; communications = internal campaigns; metrics = adoption, active use, time saved, satisfaction.
